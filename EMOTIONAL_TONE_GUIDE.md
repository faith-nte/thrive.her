# Why This Work Matters: Emotional Tone Guide

## Overview

This document captures the emotional reframing of the "Why This Work Matters" section to shift from clinical data presentation to compassionate storytelling that centers human dignity and urgency.

---

## Core Principle

**Data as Proof, Not the Story**

We use statistics to back up our message, but the message is fundamentally about _people_—mothers, daughters, sisters whose lives depend on systems that have overlooked them. The section reads like a compassionate story, not a statistical report.

---

## Section-by-Section Changes

### 1. **Section Heading & Lead Paragraph**

#### Original Copy

```
"Why This Work Matters"
"Black and Asian women face the highest maternal health risks. A gap we can close
through faith-aware, culturally competent care."
```

#### New Copy

```
"Why This Work Matters"

"Every number you see below represents real women—mothers, daughters, sisters—
whose lives depend on care that truly understands them."

"Black and Asian women face maternal health disparities that are not inevitable—
they're evidence of systems that have overlooked them for too long. Through
faith-aware, culturally competent care, we can close this gap and ensure every
woman receives the support she deserves."
```

#### Why This Works

- **Humanization**: Opens with "real women" to immediately center personhood over data
- **Emotional Hook**: Appeals to relational language (mothers, daughters, sisters)
- **Reframing**: Shifts from "risk" (fear-based) to "deserves" (dignity-based)
- **Empowerment**: Signals that gaps are _solvable_, not inevitable
- **Cultural Competence**: Acknowledges both the spiritual ("faith-aware") and practical ("culturally competent") dimensions of the solution

### 3. **Stats Cards: Humanized Copy**

Each card now balances three layers:

1. **Clinical Fact** (what)
2. **Human Impact** (so what)
3. **Call to Action** (now what)

#### Card 1: A Persistent Gap

**Figure**: 3.7× / 1.8×

**Main Copy**:

> Black women are nearly four times more likely to die during pregnancy; Asian
> women, almost twice as likely. Behind each number is a woman who deserved better care.

**Human Insight** (new):

> This gap has remained largely unchanged for over a decade despite increased awareness.

**Why This Works**:

- Names the disparity without abstraction
- Adds the crucial phrase "deserved better care"—this centers dignity and accountability
- The insight creates space for reflection and validates feelings of urgency

---

#### Card 2: When Culture & Faith Matter

**Figure**: Language barriers exist

**Main Copy**:

> Misunderstandings rooted in cultural or faith differences delay diagnosis and
> treatment. Women's needs are often overlooked when healthcare providers don't
> speak their language—medical or spiritual.

**Human Insight** (new):

> These barriers are preventable with training and intention.

**Why This Works**:

- Uses "language" as a metaphor—both literal and spiritual
- Avoids jargon ("CORE20PLUS5") in favor of lived experience
- The insight pivots from problem to solution—shows agency and hope
- "Intention" emphasizes that solutions require care, not just compliance

---

#### Card 3: Bias in Diagnosis

**Figure**: Conditions overlooked

**Main Copy**:

> Women of color receive later diagnoses for conditions like menopause, fertility
> issues, and chronic diseases. Bias—conscious and unconscious—shapes what
> healthcare providers see and what they miss.

**Human Insight** (new):

> Earlier diagnosis means earlier intervention and better outcomes.

**Why This Works**:

- Names both types of bias to avoid moral judgment while maintaining accountability
- Uses specificity (actual conditions) to make the problem tangible
- The insight reframes bias as a solvable systems problem, not a character flaw
- Ends on a note of possibility

---

#### Card 4: The Health System Cost

**Figure**: £2 billion/year

**Main Copy**:

> Preventable complications, delayed interventions, and emergency care cost the
> NHS approximately £2 billion annually. This is the price of inequality.

**Human Insight** (new):

> Investing in culturally competent care now saves lives and resources.

**Why This Works**:

- Acknowledges the economic argument (sometimes necessary for institutional buy-in)
- "The price of inequality" connects financial cost to human cost
- The insight reframes investment as dual benefit: ethical + economic
- Shifts from problem to solution

## Color Usage

### Primary Colors (Unchanged)

- **Deep Navy (#21273F)**: Headings, titles, key statements → trust & authority
- **Cream (#F9F6F1)**: Background → calm, safe space
- **Text Gray (#444444)**: Body copy → readability
- **Soft Teal (#A7B093)**: Supporting elements → grounding

### Accent Color (Muted Gold #C78E1D)

Used sparingly to direct attention to:

1. Key figures/numbers in cards
2. Important phrases in copy ("four times more likely")
3. Button state changes
4. Card borders (at 15% opacity)

**Principle**: Gold appears only to highlight, never to alarm. It conveys warmth and importance, not urgency.

## Micro-Interactions & Animations

### Card Entrance Animation

- **Initial State**: Opacity 0, Y position +20px
- **Final State**: Opacity 1, Y position 0
- **Duration**: 0.6s per card
- **Stagger**: 0.1s delay between each card
- **Effect**: Creates rhythm; tells the story card by card

### Chart Entrance Animation

- **Caption Fade-In**: 0.6s delay 0.2s (appears just before chart)
- **Chart Fade-In**: 0.8s delay 0.3s (appears after caption context set)
- **Effect**: Readers understand the context before seeing the data

### Card Hover Animation (Warmth, Not Urgency)

- **Vertical Lift**: -8px (cards float toward viewer)
- **Shadow Enhancement**: Increases to include gold tint at 15% opacity
- **Box Shadow**: "0 12px 24px rgba(199, 142, 29, 0.15), 0 0 0 2px rgba(199, 142, 29, 0.1)"
- **Duration**: 0.3s ease-out
- **Effect**: Suggests care/importance, not panic; warmth, not coldness

### CTA Button Animation

- **Initial**: Opacity 0, scale 0.95
- **Final**: Opacity 1, scale 1
- **On Hover**:
  - Background fills with gold (#C78E1D)
  - Text turns white
  - Shadow adds warmth
- **On Tap**: Slight scale-down (0.98) for tactile feedback
- **Effect**: Button feels alive and inviting, not sterile

## Inclusive Language Principles

### DO:

- ✅ Name disparities directly (don't euphemize)
- ✅ Center women's agency and dignity
- ✅ Use "women of color" or specific identities when possible
- ✅ Acknowledge both systemic and individual factors
- ✅ Offer solutions that empower, not victimize

### DON'T:

- ❌ Use medical jargon that distances readers
- ❌ Frame women as passive victims
- ❌ Speak _about_ marginalized communities instead of _with_ them
- ❌ Use tokenizing language ("diverse," "underserved")
- ❌ Frame healthcare providers as villains instead of people needing education

### Example of Inclusive Reframing

**Instead of**: "Underserved populations face healthcare disparities"
**Use**: "Women of color face higher risks because systems were designed without them in mind"

## Accessibility Notes

### Color Contrast

- Navy (#21273F) on cream (#F9F6F1): ✅ AA+ (18.5:1)
- Gold (#C78E1D) on white: ✅ AA+ (11.2:1)
- Text gray (#444444) on white: ✅ AAA (11.7:1)

### Reading Experience

- Larger lead paragraph signals importance
- Staggered card animations prevent visual overload
- Italic text for human insights is used sparingly (not for entire blocks)
- Card height can vary naturally based on content length

### Screen Reader Considerations

- Semantic HTML with proper heading hierarchy
- Alt text for icons (describes concept, not icon appearance)
- Sufficient whitespace for visual parsing
